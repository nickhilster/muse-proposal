# Bob the Bouncer — Demo Visual Storyboard v1

**Status:** Direction approved; visual timing provisional pending voiceover reconciliation.
**Purpose:** Show Bob helping one person through three scam moments, in plain language, with the same calm presence across languages.

## Visual spine

Stay near the person's phone and choices. The scam enters from outside the frame; Bob makes room to pause, inspect, and choose. The emotional movement is **ordinary morning → uncertainty → visible evidence → safe action → reassurance → agency**.

Bob remains the same full-kit character throughout. His state is carried by a soft halo and clear interface state, not by changing into another character. Yellow means watchful uncertainty; amber means a concern was found; green means the person has completed a safe step. No flashing red, alarm graphics, hacker imagery, or panic sound design.

## Storyboard

The target times below follow Muse's two-minute script map. They are editorial placeholders, not a locked edit timeline.

### 1. Start inside the moment — target 0:00–0:12

- **Picture:** A lived-in morning, framed simply: phone on a table, daylight, a hand nearby. A new email arrives. We see the sender and subject before the camera moves closer.
- **Action:** The thumb pauses. A quiet amber edge appears around the phone; Bob enters the edge of frame, steady and unhurried.
- **Feeling:** This is familiar, and there is time to think.
- **Transition:** A slow move into the email, following the user's attention.
- **Text:** No opening title. Keep the email itself legible in localized UI layers.

### 2. Show the evidence — target 0:12–0:30

- **Picture:** The message fills most of frame. The sender address, generic greeting, and destination link receive separate, generous highlights—one at a time.
- **Action:** The person taps “Why?” Bob's answer pairs each concern with the exact part of the email it came from. A safe path appears after the evidence.
- **Feeling:** Bob can explain his warning; the person does not have to take it on faith.
- **Transition:** The evidence highlights gather into a small, tidy summary beside the message.
- **Text:** Localize all interface copy. Do not bake English labels into the shared video master.

### 3. Let the moment settle — target 0:30–0:40

- **Picture:** The safe action is completed. The amber edge softens to green; the email is visibly handled.
- **Action:** Bob stays beside the phone. The interface clears space instead of adding a victory screen.
- **Feeling:** The task is finished; the person is back in control.
- **Transition:** Match the green status light to the incoming-call indicator in the next scene.

### 4. Stay through the call — target 0:40–0:55

- **Picture:** The phone displays an unknown caller. The person’s hand returns to frame. Bob is present beside the call controls, not as a wall between the person and the phone.
- **Action:** Yellow signals “not sure yet.” Bob asks to be put on speaker; the person taps the speaker control.
- **Feeling:** The person is accompanied, not rushed.
- **Sound:** A soft, brief vibration cue only; no loud ring or threatening sting.

### 5. Make the next steps easy — target 0:55–1:15

- **Picture:** Three large touch targets fill the phone screen. We see the person tap through without typing. An amber state appears with the reason and the safe action.
- **Action:** “Hang up” is prominent. The person chooses it; a verified CRA contact card is then available as a one-tap path.
- **Feeling:** Even under stress, the next safe action is clear.
- **Text:** Use only a verified official contact path. Keep the number itself out of the storyboard until confirmed against the current official source.

### 6. State the digital-arrest truth — target 1:15–1:20 and 1:20–1:40

- **Picture:** A video-call screen shows a caller performing authority, with a fake warrant as a small inset. Keep the caller's screen visually busy but not frightening; avoid portraying real police or a real victim as the threat.
- **Action:** The busy frame recedes. Bob remains clear in the foreground while one plain-language statement occupies the visual focus. Hold long enough to read it in every language.
- **Feeling:** The scam uses fear and authority; Bob calmly names the lie.
- **Transition:** The isolated statement becomes the first card in the three-state sequence.
- **Text:** The legal sentence and any localized wording must match the reviewed script exactly.

### 7. Make the system visible — target 1:40–1:50

- **Picture:** Three large, separated cards appear in order: yellow, amber, green. Each uses its color plus a distinct icon and localized label, so meaning does not depend on color alone.
- **Action:** A thin line connects alert → evidence → safe step. The case summary moves toward the person, who taps “Done.”
- **Feeling:** Bob organizes what matters; the person closes the loop.
- **Transition:** The line resolves into the outline of the phone in the closing shot.

### 8. Leave Bob with the person — target 1:50–2:00

- **Picture:** Full-kit Bob beside the phone, at the same human scale as before. The language menu is visible with EN, MR, FR, and ES choices; the selected language is clear.
- **Action:** The menu settles; Bob gives a small reassuring nod. End on the user-facing tagline and hold the final image rather than cutting away quickly.
- **Feeling:** One Bob, one calm voice, available in the viewer's language.
- **Text:** Localize the tagline and menu. Keep captions on a separate language-specific layer.

## Production guardrails

- Keep one focus per shot, large interface elements, generous reading time, and steady camera movement.
- The interface is a clickable prototype. Show a small, localized “Prototype demo” marker while product screens are visible; do not imply live email ingestion, call interception, or automated verdicts.
- The single voice is locked across EN, MR, FR, and ES. Do not add character voices or switch voices within a language track.
- Localized UI text, captions, and the final menu must remain replaceable so the picture master does not privilege English.
- Use the full-kit reference at `/home/nikhil/dev/muse-proposal/assets/characters/bob-security-full-kit.webp`; the earlier cream-fur/SECURITY-shirt concept in this repo is not the locked final look.

## Timing gate

That does not leave enough spoken time for the mapped two-minute pacing, and the tracks do not share a common duration. Treat the shot order and visual progression as approved; retime the board only after the voice tracks are confirmed or re-recorded to shared beat cues.
