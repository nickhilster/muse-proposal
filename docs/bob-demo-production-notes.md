# Bob the Bouncer — approved demo production direction

**Status:** Working production direction, based on Nikhil's approval on 2026-10-17.
**Source package:** [Muse's original demo package](bob-demo-package.md) remains unchanged.

## Voice direction

The demo must use at least three distinct Muse voices. The purpose is to show that
Bob's calm, clear help can be delivered in voices that feel right to different
people. Muse's one-voice proposal is not the approved direction.

Use three clearly distinct voice identities for the same Bob persona in the
English master. Keep Bob's behavior, language, and visual identity consistent;
let the selected voice change between scenario chapters. Each voice stays warm,
plain-spoken, unhurried, and nonjudgmental. Do not make the threat scenes sound
more dramatic than the resolution.

### Casting map for the two-minute cut

| Time | Voice | Scenario / role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:40 | Voice A | Opening premise and phishing email |
| 0:40–1:15 | Voice B | Unknown call and CRA safe path |
| 1:15–2:00 | Voice C | India digital-arrest warning, shared system, close |

Show a brief “Bob voice 1 / 2 / 3” selection cue at chapter transitions. This
makes voice choice part of the product story, rather than making the cast sound
like three unrelated narrators. The final voice names and exact Muse presets are
open until the script and voice samples are reviewed.

## Script adaptation notes

Use Muse's time-coded script in the source package as the starting copy. Preserve
its core beats and proof points while assigning the lines to the casting map
above. Keep the first-person Bob narration where it helps the character feel
present. Any line that implies a real detection, call interception, or email
integration must be clearly presented as a prototype scenario; the current
prototypes are scripted simulations.

For the first cut, record one English master and subtitle it. If localized audio
is added later, give each language its own casting decision and beat-timed read;
do not assume one English voice can or should represent every language. Keep
captions and on-screen text as editable overlays so they can be localized without
re-rendering the visuals.

## Production checklist

- Record three distinct Bob voice samples against the same short line before
  locking the final cast.
- Adapt the source script to the three-voice chapter map and review the complete
  read against the two-minute timecodes.
- Screen-record each clickable prototype for its assigned scenario.
- Make the simulated status and scripted verdict clear in the final video.
- Build the title cards for the yellow / amber / green states and agent-to-human
  handoff.
- Use the full-kit character art for the on-duty close; keep the teacher
  character for an off-duty learning follow-up.
- Keep the final music and sound mix calm and legible under all three voices.
